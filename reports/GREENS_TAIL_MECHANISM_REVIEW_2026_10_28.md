# Greens tail audit: assess the mechanism, not a seat-count cap

## Main finding

A 0–9 Greens seat interval is not inherently unreasonable. No seat-count cap or
restriction has been introduced. However, some outcomes in the experimental
uniform-width 0.30 trial are generated by a spatial transform that **reverses
the initial strength ranking**, rather than merely weakening or flattening it.
That is a structural assumption requiring justification, not evidence of a more
rigorous uncertainty forecast. It should not have remained hidden in summaries.

This audit preserves the trial's outputs and adds instrumentation/warnings;
it does not retrospectively remove unfavourable draws or change defaults.

## Replay and sample

Replayed every archived uniform-0.30 draw where Greens won zero or at least nine
seats: 35 draws out of 200 (16 zero, 19 at least nine). These are selected tails,
not a new random sample for estimating probabilities. Full party seat counts
match the archive in every replay. The archived 5th–95th range was 0–9; the
maximum observed count was 14, so nine was not a hard upper bound.

Underlying trial: saved ALP 24.6 / LNP 29.1 / GRN 13.3 / ON 22 / IND 5.5 /
OTH 5.5; all-party concentration SD 0.30; upper-house regional noise, no new
regional preference trial; committed inputs, two seeds, 100 draws each. The
statewide target varies within simulations. These are not current polling inputs.

## Why reversal is possible

For positive base share b, current concentration tilt is:

`b * exp(c * (log(b) - mean_log_b) / sd_log_b)`

Ignoring the common multiplicative constant, this equals:

`b ** (1 + c / sd_log_b)`

Thus a sufficiently negative coefficient c produces a negative power. Before
balancing with other parties, initially stronger seats become weaker and vice
versa. Balancing and other party/local errors can also change final rankings;
this diagnostic specifically identifies reversal already present in the transform.

Ten of the sixteen zero-Greens draws have a negative Greens initial power.
The other six have positive powers but strong flattening combined with other
conditions. Zero-seat draws have effective powers from -0.788 to 0.431, with
median -0.108. The >=9-seat draws have powers from 1.248 to 2.806.

## Concrete examples

### Zero Greens seats despite 15.09% statewide primary

Seed 20261010, draw 8: Greens concentration coefficient -0.757 and effective
power -0.788. Statewide Greens primary remains 15.09%, but district primaries
include Brunswick 8.56%, Melbourne 9.45%, Richmond 8.16%, Prahran 10.77%.
The largest Greens primary anywhere is 24.04%. These figures arise from the
sampled spatial transform and balancing, not a lower statewide Greens target.

Holding the same target and other parameter/local errors:

- Remove only the Greens concentration factor: six Greens seats.
- Remove all concentration factors: seven Greens seats.
- Keep primaries but remove preference perturbations: still zero Greens seats.

This is evidence that the primary spatial mechanism drives this draw, not a
claim that a real election could never produce zero Greens seats.

### Fourteen Greens seats

Seed 20261010, draw 3: statewide Greens 16.62%, coefficient +0.699 and effective
power 2.806. Brunswick Greens primary is 84.49%, Melbourne 80.85%, Richmond
75.85%, Prahran 66.15%. Greens win fourteen seats, including some marginal
non-core contests. The extreme local shares warrant substantive review; a high
seat count itself is not a sufficient reason to reject the outcome.

Remove only the Greens concentration factor: eight seats. Remove all concentration:
eight. Keep primaries but remove preference perturbations: twelve.

### Nine Greens seats on 11.30% statewide primary

Seed 20261011, draw 26: positive concentration coefficient +0.512 and effective
power above one. Greens win nine seats through concentrated inner-city strength
and particular final-pair paths, including Brighton and Caulfield in this draw.
This shows why the same statewide vote need not map to a fixed seat count.
It does not establish the predictive frequency or realism of those specific paths.

## Counterfactual summary

For the sixteen zero-seat draws:

| Counterfactual | Greens seats: min / median / max |
|---|---|
| Greens concentration factor off | 4 / 5 / 7 |
| All concentration factors off | 4 / 5 / 7 |
| Preference perturbations off | 0 / 0 / 3 |

For the nineteen >=9-seat draws:

| Counterfactual | Greens seats: min / median / max |
|---|---|
| Greens concentration factor off | 5 / 7 / 9 |
| All concentration factors off | 5 / 6 / 9 |
| Preference perturbations off | 6 / 9 / 12 |

The counterfactuals preserve targets, candidate/parameter conditions and paired
local shocks. All-concentration-off retains central ON alpha as in the tail
trial, so it is not a complete replay of the ordinary forecast baseline.
Other-party concentration can still change Greens shares through composition.

## Instrumentation now added

Concentration forecast runs record the effective initial power for each party
and draw. Run details display minimum/maximum powers and negative-power counts,
with a warning if reversals occur. A diagnostic CSV is downloadable. This does
not reject, clip, resample or alter those draws. Defaults/Main remain unchanged.

22 targeted tests pass, including an explicit exponent diagnostic test; the
test uses exactly the same log-SD convention as the production strength contrast.
The UI warning is smoke-tested. Full suite: 117 pass and the same pre-existing
legacy-workbook fixture failure remains. Hosted rebuild not independently checked.

high ON support. Source discovery is not calibration.

## Recommended next trial

Separate ordinary persistence uncertainty from explicit wholesale realignment.
Test a positive-persistence formulation that permits weaker/stronger concentration
without silently turning strongholds into weakholds. Do not choose arbitrary
seat-count floors or upper bounds. A positive initial exponent does not guarantee
unchanged final rankings after all other effects, nor does it prohibit zero or
nine Greens seats.

Compare matched central behaviour and tail severity, not merely the same numeric
SD under different parameterisations. Positive/lognormal persistence can itself
have an excessive upper tail or move the median/mean, so it must be tested,
not presumed a fix. If wholesale geographic reversal is to be included, label
it as a separate scenario and justify its probability rather than obtaining it
accidentally from an unrestricted normal coefficient.
